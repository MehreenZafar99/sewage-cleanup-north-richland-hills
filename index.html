Why Sewage Backups Happen in NRH

Property age, soil, and storms shape the backups we see across North Richland Hills.

Ask a pro →
01

Older neighborhoods

in the southern part of the city, near Loop 820 and Boulevard 26, have many homes built in the 1950s, 60s and 70s. Their sewer lines are often clay or cast iron. Clay joints let tree roots in, and cast iron rusts and scales on the inside until waste catches and builds up.

↗
02

Newer neighborhoods

in the north, near North Tarrant Parkway and Precinct Line Road, mostly have PVC lines. Roots are less of an issue there, but wipes, grease and paper towels still cause blockages.

↗
03

North Texas clay soil

is a factor everywhere in the city. It swells when wet and shrinks during dry summers. That movement can shift, crack or sag a sewer line over time, leaving low spots where waste collects.

↗
04

Heavy rain

can push extra water into the system and cause backups through the lowest drain in a home, which is usually a shower or tub on a slab.

↗

What To Do Right Now

If sewage is coming into your property, these first steps protect your family and your floors:

  • ✓ Stop using water. Do not flush toilets, run the dishwasher, shower or do laundry. Every gallon you send down the drain can come back up.
  • ✓ Keep people and pets out. Close doors to the affected rooms. Children, older adults and anyone with a weak immune system should stay away completely.
  • ✓ Do not touch it with bare hands. Sewage contains E. coli, hepatitis A and other germs that enter through small cuts and splashes.
  • ✓ Turn off power to wet areas if you can reach the breaker safely without standing in water.
  • ✓ Call (817) 204-3891. We can talk you through the next few minutes while the crew heads your way.
