Sewage Backup Cleanup in North Richland Hills

A sewage backup happens when wastewater cannot flow out to the city sewer, so it comes back into the property instead. It almost always appears at the lowest drain first. In a slab home that is usually a shower, tub or ground floor toilet. In a business it may be a floor drain or a restroom.

Signs You Have a Sewage Backup

If more than one fixture is affected, the blockage is usually in the main line, not a single drain.

  • ✓ Dark, smelly water rising in a tub or shower when a toilet is flushed or a washing machine drains
  • ✓ Gurgling sounds from drains or toilets
  • ✓ Several drains running slowly at the same time
  • ✓ Water coming out of a sewer cleanout in the yard

Common Causes in NRH

Tree roots. Older neighborhoods in the south part of the city have large oaks and pecans and original clay sewer lines. Roots grow into the joints and catch waste.

Corroded cast iron. Homes from the 1950s through the 1970s often have cast iron under the slab. Over decades the inside surface scales and narrows the pipe.

Clay soil movement. North Texas clay swells in wet months and shrinks in summer. That movement can crack or sag a line, creating low spots called bellies where waste collects.

Why Speed Matters

Sewage starts soaking into drywall and wood within minutes. Within 24 to 48 hours, bacteria multiply and mold can begin growing on wet materials. A backup handled in the first few hours often needs only the lower part of a wall removed. One left over a weekend can mean full flooring and cabinet replacement.
