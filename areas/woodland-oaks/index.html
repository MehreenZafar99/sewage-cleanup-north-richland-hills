How Clay Soil Damages Sewer Lines

North Texas clay swells when it gets wet and shrinks when it dries out. After a long hot summer with little rain, the soil pulls away and cracks open. When fall rains return, it swells back up. This cycle repeats every year.

Sewer lines buried in that soil move with it. Over time, the movement can:

Big oak trees make it worse. During a drought, their roots spread further looking for water, and a leaking sewer joint is an easy target.

  • ✓ Pull joints apart, letting roots and soil in
  • ✓ Crack the pipe, especially older clay or cast iron
  • ✓ Create sags (bellies) where waste settles instead of flowing
  • ✓ Shift the line out of proper slope

When Backups Peak

We often see more sewer problems in Woodland Oaks type neighborhoods:

  • ✓ Late summer and early fall, after months of dry, shrinking soil and thirsty roots
