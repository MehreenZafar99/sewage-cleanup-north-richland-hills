Trees and Older Sewer Lines

Many Diamond Loch homes were built decades ago, when sewer lines were often made of clay or cast iron. Clay pipe comes in short sections joined together, and over time those joints loosen. Tree roots sense the moisture inside and grow in.

Once inside, roots:

Large oaks, pecans and other mature trees on Diamond Loch lots can send roots a long way, so a tree that looks far from the house can still reach the sewer line.

  • ✓ Catch toilet paper and waste, slowly closing off the pipe
  • ✓ Grow larger each year, which can crack or break the pipe
  • ✓ Come back after being cut out unless the line is repaired or lined

Longer Lines on Larger Lots

Bigger lots often mean a longer sewer lateral from the house to the city main. More pipe means more joints, more places for roots and more chances for a sag. When a backup happens, a plumber's camera can find where the problem is along that length.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching these early can mean a plumber clears the line before sewage ever enters the house.

  • ✓ Toilets that gurgle or bubble
  • ✓ Several drains running slowly at the same time
  • ✓ Water rising in a tub when the washer drains
  • ✓ Wet, soggy or extra green patches of lawn above the sewer line
  • ✓ A sewage smell outdoors near the cleanout
