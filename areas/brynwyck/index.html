Hardwood Floors

Solid hardwood can sometimes be saved after a sewage backup, depending on how quickly the sewage was removed and how deep it went into the boards. Engineered hardwood is harder to save because its layered core can swell and separate.

We look at:

If boards can be saved, they are cleaned, disinfected and dried with specialized floor drying systems. If not, we document them so your adjuster can see why.

  • ✓ How long the sewage sat on the wood
  • ✓ Whether sewage got between or under the boards
  • ✓ Moisture levels in the wood and subfloor
  • ✓ Whether boards are cupping or swelling

Natural Stone and Tile

Marble, travertine and other natural stone are porous. Sewage can soak in and stain. Sealed stone in good condition can often be cleaned and disinfected. Unsealed or damaged stone may need professional restoration or replacement.

Rugs, Furniture and Belongings

We help you sort every item and photograph it for your insurance claim.

  • ✓ Area rugs: Fine wool or hand knotted rugs that touched sewage should go to a specialist rug cleaner. Synthetic rugs that soaked through are usually discarded.
  • ✓ Wood furniture: Solid wood pieces can usually be cleaned and refinished.
  • ✓ Upholstered furniture: Usually discarded if soaked, since the padding cannot be fully disinfected.
  • ✓ Artwork, documents and keepsakes: Move them out of the area right away if it is safe. Specialists can sometimes restore items that were splashed.
