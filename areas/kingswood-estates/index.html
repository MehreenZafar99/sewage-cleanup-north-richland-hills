How Grease Causes Sewage Backups

Bacon fat, cooking oil, butter, gravy and meat drippings go down the drain as a warm liquid. Once inside the pipe, they cool and harden. Over months and years, grease coats the inside of the line, and food scraps and coffee grounds stick to it until the line is nearly closed off.

When it finally blocks:

  • ✓ Dirty water fills the kitchen sink and does not drain
  • ✓ The dishwasher backs up onto the floor
  • ✓ If the blockage is further down, in the main line, sewage can come up at the lowest drain in the house

Holidays Are Peak Time

Thanksgiving and the winter holidays are some of the busiest days of the year for kitchen and sewer backups. More cooking means more grease, more guests mean more toilets in use, and more dishes mean more dishwasher loads.

Tips to avoid a holiday backup:

  • ✓ Pour cooled grease into a can or jar and throw it in the trash
  • ✓ Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing
  • ✓ Scrape plates into the trash, not the sink
  • ✓ Do not put potato peels, rice or pasta down the garbage disposal
  • ✓ Run the dishwasher after guests leave, not during peak toilet use

Kitchen Backups and Cabinets

When a kitchen line backs up, water runs under the sink base, soaks the cabinet floor and spreads under the cabinets and toe kicks. Many cabinet bases are particle board, which swells and holds contamination. Water can also reach under the dishwasher and refrigerator.
