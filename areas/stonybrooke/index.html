The Problem With Drains That Sit Unused

Every drain has a U shaped pipe called a P trap that holds a small amount of water. That water blocks sewer gas from coming into the room. When a shower, tub or floor drain is not used for weeks, the water evaporates and sewer gas comes straight in. The fix can be as simple as running water for a minute.

A slow leak under a guest toilet or behind a rarely used shower can go on for months because nobody is in that room to see it. By the time someone notices a smell or a soft floor, the subfloor and wall may be badly damaged.

When the main line is blocked, sewage comes up at the lowest drain in the house. That is often a guest shower or tub, so a backup can sit in a closed off bathroom for days before anyone opens the door.

A Simple Monthly Habit

Once a month, run water for 30 seconds in every sink, tub, shower and floor drain, and flush every toilet. Look around the base of each toilet and under each sink. This keeps traps full and helps you catch leaks early.

When It Is More Than a Dry Trap

Call us if the smell stays after you run water, or if you see:

  • ✓ Stains or soft spots around the toilet base
  • ✓ Swollen baseboards or peeling paint near a tub
  • ✓ Dark water in a tub or shower drain
  • ✓ Mold spots on the lower wall
