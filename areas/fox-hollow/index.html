Why a Backup That Sat for Days Is Different

Sewage caught in the first few hours mostly sits on the surface. Sewage that sat for days:

The cleanup is bigger, but it is still very doable with the right approach.

  • ✓ Wicks higher up the walls, often 12 to 24 inches above the floor
  • ✓ Soaks deeper into subfloor, slab edges and framing
  • ✓ Grows bacteria to much higher levels
  • ✓ Starts mold growth, which can begin in 24 to 48 hours on wet materials
  • ✓ Saturates the air with odor that gets into fabrics and furniture

How a Backup Can Happen While You Are Away

You might think no water use means no backup. But:

  • ✓ A neighbor's water use or a city main problem can push sewage back into your line
  • ✓ Heavy rain can overload the system
  • ✓ A toilet with a stuck fill valve can run for days and overflow
  • ✓ An ice maker or washer line can leak and turn into contaminated standing water

What To Do When You Find It

  • ✓ Do not walk through the sewage. Back out of the area.
  • ✓ Open windows if the smell is very strong, but stay out of the affected rooms.
  • ✓ Turn off the HVAC so it does not spread contaminated air.
  • ✓ Shut off the main water if a fixture is running.
  • ✓ Take photos from the doorway.
